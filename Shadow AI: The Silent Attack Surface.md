### The Risk That Enters the Organization Quietly, Silently and Completely Voluntarily

A new class of high-level threats is emerging through the uninformed use of artificial intelligence.

This is not about a single platform. Every day, dozens of new AI services appear many unknown, some unreliable. A significant number of them lack clear infrastructure transparency, defined privacy models, identifiable data processing locations, or even a verifiable legal entity behind them.

For years, we spoke about Shadow IT software introduced into organizations without the knowledge of the security team, creating unseen risk.

Today, we are facing something more serious: **Shadow AI**.

This time, it’s not just about installing unauthorized software. Your employee, your developer, even your executive may voluntarily submit organizational data directly into an AI engine.

And that is precisely where the risk begins.

---

### What Is Shadow AI?

Shadow AI occurs when employees use online AI tools to work faster or solve problems more efficiently without those tools being reviewed or approved by the security or IT department.

No contract or confidentiality agreement has been assessed. No risk assessment has been conducted. No clear policy defines what data is allowed to be shared.

For example:

- A developer pastes real production server logs into an AI tool for debugging.
- An analyst uploads a file containing customer information for summarization.
- A manager submits an internal contract for rewriting or refinement.

No data sanitization. No masking. No classification.

Yet real, operational, and sometimes mission-critical data leaves the organization’s controlled boundary disguised as a simple “chat.” And that simplicity is exactly what hides the risk.

---

### What Types of Data Are at Risk?

Many people assume, “It’s just a chat.”

From a security perspective, however, these interactions can become an aggregation point of sensitive enterprise data.

In practice, individuals often submit the following in pursuit of faster answers:

- Full production server logs
- Internal source code snippets
- Database structures
- Real user data samples
- API keys and authentication tokens
- Contract documentation
- Internal correspondence
- Financial analyses
- Other confidential and regulated information

The user is simply trying to get better results.

From a security standpoint, this is a direct transfer of sensitive corporate data to a third-party service.

---

### Why Is Shadow AI More Dangerous Than Shadow IT?

**1. It Is Entirely Voluntary**

There is no malware. There is no intrusion.

The employee acts with positive intent to improve efficiency or solve a problem quickly. The threat originates internally, driven by productivity, not by external attackers.

**2. Extremely Rapid Adoption**

AI tools require no installation. A browser is enough.

**3. Lack of Transparency and Data Governance**

For many AI services, it is unclear:

- Where is the data stored?
- How long is it retained?
- Who has access to it?
- Is it used for model training?

Without transparent answers, effective risk management becomes nearly impossible.

**4. High Data Concentration**

Users tend to describe problems comprehensively including context, dependencies, architecture details, and sometimes real production data.

Information is not just transferred. It is structured, contextualized, and made readable.

From an attacker’s perspective, this is a classified intelligence repository.

---

### Questions Every Organization Should Be Asking

- Is the use of AI tools governed by a formal framework, or left to individual discretion?
- Is data classified and sanitized before submission to AI platforms?
- Is AI-related traffic monitored and logged?
- Do employees clearly understand what type of data must never be shared?
- Do existing DLP and Secure Web Gateway controls cover AI web applications, or are they outside visibility?

If the answers are unclear, Shadow AI is likely already active within your organization.

---

### The Reality

We have always worried about external breaches.

But with Shadow AI, data exfiltration begins internally normal, routine, and completely silent.

Major breaches rarely start with sophisticated zero-day exploits. They often begin with a simple thought:

“Let me ask the AI and see what it says.”

Just as email and cloud platforms became attack vectors years ago, AI interactions can now become a new source of data leakage arguably more dangerous, because users voluntarily and confidently provide structured information.

Security is not only about stopping attacks.

Many breaches begin with everyday behavior.

If AI usage in your organization is not classified, logged, or governed you may already be building a silent risk layer.

AI is a competitive advantage. Without data governance, it becomes a security blind spot.

---

### AI Is a Competitive Advantage - But Only With Governance

This is not about opposing artificial intelligence.

It is about managing it.

Organizations that fail to establish governance frameworks for AI today may face future data leaks that originated from something as simple as a conversation.

Shadow AI is not a theoretical threat. It is a new attack surface.

And in many organizations, it is already expanding unnoticed.
